Business problem

Construction RFIs and change orders move through reviews, handoffs, revisions, pricing, and approval gates. Weak workflow visibility can create delayed decisions, unpriced work, forecast uncertainty, rework, and commercial exposure.

Primary question

Which change-order causes and RFI workflow conditions create the greatest cost and schedule exposure, and where should management intervene?

All projects, companies, people, budgets, schedules, RFIs, and changes are synthetic. No actual client or confidential record is represented.

Analytical lifecycle

The project follows Ask → Prepare → Process → Analyze → Share → Act, with terminology and practices aligned to CRISP-DM, DMAIC, Microsoft’s data-science lifecycle, and PMI project-governance process groups.

Key findings

Decision speed affects both workflows. Project-average RFI response and change approval cycle produced Pearson r = 0.817.
Digital coordination matters. High digital-coordination projects averaged 9.55 response days and 62.0% on-time performance.
Coordination conflicts created the largest RFI cost impact. They generated approximately $8.23 million and 533 schedule-impact days.
Commercial exposure is concentrated. Owner-directed, design error/omission, and unforeseen-condition changes produced approximately $124.28 million in approved value.

Management priority model

The project uses seven transparent indicators covering response time, overdue backlog, High/Critical RFIs, approval cycle, pending exposure, forecast lag, and approved items not incorporated into forecasts.

Project health: 29 Red, 48 Yellow, and 13 Green.

The model is deterministic and explainable. It supports management prioritization, not legal or contractual conclusions.
